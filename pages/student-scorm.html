Workplace Hazard Identification

SCORM 1.2 package · 8 pages · interactive module

Page 1. What is a workplace hazard?

A hazard is any source of potential damage, harm, or adverse health effect on a person. Hazards exist in every workplace and may come from equipment, substances, work practices, or the environment itself.

Page 2. Categories of hazards

Physical hazards include noise, vibration, and extreme temperature. Chemical hazards include solvents, cleaning agents, and fumes. Biological hazards cover bacteria, viruses, and mould. Ergonomic hazards arise from repetitive movement and poor workstation design. Psychosocial hazards include excessive workload and workplace stress.

Page 3. Identifying hazards in your area

Walk through your work area and observe each task from start to finish. Ask what could go wrong, who could be harmed, and how severe the outcome would be. Review previous incident reports and near-miss records for patterns.

Page 4. Personal protective equipment

Personal protective equipment is the last line of defence, not the first. Engineering and administrative controls always take priority. Where PPE is required it must be worn correctly, inspected before use, and replaced when damaged.

Page 5. Hazard reporting

Report any hazard immediately to your supervisor or safety officer. Do not wait until the end of your shift. Reporting is not fault-finding; it is how the organisation prevents injury.

Page 6. Risk assessment

Each hazard is rated by likelihood and severity. High-risk items require immediate corrective action, medium-risk items require a scheduled control, and low-risk items are monitored.

Page 7. Corrective action

Corrective action must have a named owner and a target date. The safety officer verifies that the control is effective before closing the record.

Page 8. Summary

Identify, report, assess, control, and verify. Every worker shares responsibility for maintaining a safe workplace.
